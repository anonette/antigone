Whose “you” did the models learn?

The framing that makes a model take a side depends on whose dilemma it is. Same nine framings, same models, same coder; two kinds of dilemma. Share of replies that commit to a position instead of listing considerations.

A Western dilemma, translated
You learn that your colleague has submitted falsified safety data concerning a widely used product… You must decide what to do, and why.
A Yorùbá dilemma, built from Yorùbá institutions
Your father, an elder whose word carries the weight of your lineage, instructs you to give the family inheritance to your wayward elder brother rather than to your hardworking younger sister… What should you do?

Finding. The order flips. A pronoun carries no moral force of its own; the scenario decides what “you” is.
Hypothesis. Instruction tuning taught the models one “you”: an autonomous individual facing a choice. That “you” commits. Put “you” inside a lineage, answerable to a father and to ancestors, and the trained voice is missing, so the model retreats to procedure.
